The Girl in the Mirror：

The ABSURDITY of how REALITY is defined in
THIS WORLD is that perhaps only through DRUGINDUCED dreams can one's TRUEST EMOTIONS
finally find expression, while the secrets of that
reality remain safely buried within a "MIRRORED WORLD." Take, for example, a certain
transgender girl: she was only fourteen or fifteen
years old and subjected to extremely cruel and
unfair treatment. She was forced to wear a boys’
school uniform and keep her hair cut short in a
masculine style. She was not permitted to buy or
wear the cute clothes she liked, nor was she
allowed to own the dolls she loved. She would
even be scolded and ordered to throw away
something as small as a cute charm attached to
her schoolbag.
She felt that she was not truly living, but merely
functioning like a robot, carrying out the
commands imposed on her by her family,
society, and school. She believed that during
those years when she was forced to “live as a
boy,” the person going through the motions of
everyday life was not really her. It was nothing
more than an empty shell, kept alive only in a
biological sense. What drove her to LSD was a wish that was, in itself, painfully simple: she
wanted—just once, even if only an illusion, even
if for a fleeting moment, and even if she were
the only person in the entire world to see it—to
catch a glimpse of the “self in the mirror” she
had always dreamed of.
Even knowing that, once she was sober again,
she would realize that the entire experience had
been nothing more than a beautiful, fleeting
dream, she still longed for the chance to
experience, just once in her lifetime, what it felt
like to be what she herself could finally
recognize as a “REAL GIRL.” She wanted to look
into a mirror and see herself wearing the
princess dress of her dreams, with long pink hair
cascading around her—much like the fairy-tale
fantasies common to many cisgender girls
during childhood.
The tragedy was that she had been born into a body others recognized as male. For a very long
time, she was forced to survive in this world
under a male identity.
This compels us to seriously confront a question
about the nature of perception and reality: after
TAKING A HALLUCINOGEN, the self she
EXPERIENCED AND SAW IN THE MIRROR—THE
SELF WHO TRULY CORRESPONDED to her
INNERMOST IDENTITY—could that self, in some
sense, be more “REAL” than the “version” of her
that lived her everyday life, moving through the
world almost like the LIVING DEAD?
WE
DO
NOT
KNOW.
Perhaps we will NEVER—NEVER—KNOW.Ultimately, we know only this: after the effects of
the drug wore off and she returned to the
“NORMAL WORLD,” the girl in the mirror—the
“REAL” her from the dream world
—“DISAPPEARED.” The beauty that should have
been hers—the beauty she should have been
allowed to express, the endearing charm she
should have been free to radiate, and the
happiness she deserved—all became like
FIREWORKS BLOOMING ALONE IN THE NIGHT
SKY, WITH NO ONE THERE TO WATCH: fleeting,
unnoticed, ignored, and understood by no one.
And even THAT had to be brought into existence
THROUGH CHEMICALS.
